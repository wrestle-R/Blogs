## My Flagship

Now, if you've ever spoken to me in the past month, I must have 100% brought up [Multi Codex](https://github.com/wrestle-R/multi-codex). In short, this is an app which some Twitter folk put as “an alternative for signing in and signing out”.

![Multi Codex account dashboard](/blogimages/october-2026/multicodex.png)

Now here's the thing, this isn't really my first desktop app, but it was my first desktop app that I really wanted to bring to other operating systems and not just Linux. And I did.

Initially, it was terrible. It was working well on Linux, but on the other OSes it was terrible. [Gavin](https://gavin-soares.vercel.app/) helped fix it on Mac a bit, but then I learnt about GitHub runners and how they provide a small instance for testing and shit. That really helped me build a semi-stable version for Windows and Mac (I was very surprised to see it work on my Windows dual boot).

I even had a real user for my app who used to raise issues and be very happy when I solved them. Which was pretty cool, like someone was actually using the thing I made and cared enough to tell me when something wasn't working.

But yeah, as soon as I was like, oh yeah, my productivity setup, workspace, yada yada was complete, [BigRom](https://romeiro.dev/) introduced me to [T3 Code](https://github.com/pingdotgg/t3code) and some [40-minute video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uLGxb4F3SyI) of Theo explaining it.

I have never switched my entire workspace to a new style quicker. I genuinely liked the Codex app (I actually waited a long time for it to come on Linux), but T3 is just sooo much better in every way. The mobile app of T3 Code, I have no words. It's just perfect. I can't even imagine something better (an imagination issue perhaps).

But yeah, I developed an app for nearly every day in a month and switched off it in less than a day. And it wasn't just an app I was using, it was made by me, for me.

## Changing with AI

It's kinda funny to look back at how much AI has changed the way I code, at least for me. I pretty much started during the peak vibe-coding era, thanks to the GitHub Student Developer Pack. This was back when GitHub Copilot was actually pretty good.

I remember writing absolutely massive prompts, dumping tonnes of context into the models, hoping they'd understand what I wanted, and praying everything would work by the second try.

The frontend was a different story altogether. Early Sonnet and Haiku models were pretty terrible at UI/UX (at least in my experience). I'd get the functionality working and then end up writing most of the CSS myself to make it look the way I wanted.

Then things got a little better. You could practically get an entire backend endpoint done in one prompt, and the UI was actually starting to look pretty decent. Still needed to manually tweak things here and there, but it was a massive improvement.

And then came Opus bhaiya.

This one was a bit of a gamble. Sometimes you'd give it one prompt and it'd build an entire feature, almost magically. Other times it'd run into some weird issue, go down a rabbit hole, and you'd still have to jump in and debug things manually.

And now? I genuinely haven't seen an error page in a long time.

I mean, these things will happily run 200 tests, some of which are probably completely irrelevant to what you're building, just to make sure nothing breaks. The 3D stuff is also ridiculously good now. At least the Codex models have been pretty impressive in this regard.

They still occasionally miss with the UI or decide to introduce some absolutely bizarre tech stack when you haven't explicitly specified one, but overall, no complaints.

I actually sorta wish I got to experience coding before the AI era (not too much though). Like reading the documentation, learning the syntax and making something on my own. Coz even now the feeling of creating something is unbelievably amazing and I can't even fathom how it would have felt back then.

The only time I really coded everything myself was during SIH, when a teammate generated a preview of a website using Wix. I sat there all day and all night and hardcoded each and every thing using HTML and CSS. It looked pretty good, there was just no functionality behind it.

Then I learnt a bit of React. Now I have never really understood JavaScript, but React with its hooks was somehow easy to understand.

At another hackathon called Bit N Build, a teammate made an entire website using Framer and I turned that into code. So I was looking at the website and trying to recreate it in React. Again, there wasn't really any functionality behind it, just clickable and hardcoded buttons and shit.
